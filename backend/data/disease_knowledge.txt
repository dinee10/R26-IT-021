Typical Symptoms: Blister-like or blotch-like mined areas, chewed or missing tissue, torn-looking portions of the leaf, yellowing or slight curling from sap feeding, sticky honeydew, white cottony mealybug masses, scale-like bumps, or fine webbing/stippling from mites. Symptoms vary with the pest.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy Kalanchoe pinnata leaves are thick, succulent and intact without feeding mines, chewing damage, abnormal yellowing/curling, honeydew, cottony pest masses or mite webbing.
Recommended Action: Do not use heavily insect-damaged, contaminated or deteriorating leaves for medicinal preparation. Prefer a separate intact healthy leaf after the infestation is controlled. Minor cosmetic injury alone does not prove that a leaf is unsafe, and an image cannot verify pesticide residues or chemical quality. Inspect leaf surfaces, margins, axils and tender growth to identify the pest. Remove heavily affected leaves and hand-remove visible larvae or colonies where practical. Sap-sucking pests can often be reduced with a firm water rinse or an appropriately labelled insecticidal soap/horticultural treatment. Use only controls registered for the intended crop/use and follow all label directions.

Species: murraya_koenigii
Disease Name: Fungus
Display Name: Fungal Infection / Fungal Leaf Disease
Description: A generic fungal-disease class for curry leaf (Murraya koenigii). The label should not be interpreted as one specific fungus unless the source dataset confirms it. A well-documented fungal leaf disease of curry leaf is anthracnose/leaf spot caused by Colletotrichum gloeosporioides; powdery mildew has also been reported on curry leaf. For a generic visual class, the safest wording is therefore 'fungal infection' rather than assigning a pathogen from the image alone.
Typical Symptoms: Depending on the fungus, symptoms may include small dark or black necrotic spots, circular lesions that enlarge and become brown, tan or black, yellow halos around lesions, coalescing blackish patches, tip blight or burning, premature leaf drop, or white powdery fungal growth in powdery-mildew cases. Colletotrichum leaf spot on curry leaf is specifically reported to begin as small circular dark spots that enlarge and may develop visible black fungal fruiting bodies.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy curry leaf leaflets are intact, evenly green and free from necrotic spots, expanding brown/black lesions, yellow lesion halos, fungal fruiting bodies, powdery surface growth, tip blight and premature drying.
Recommended Action: Do not use curry leaves with visible active fungal infection, mould-like growth, substantial necrotic spotting or blight for medicinal preparation. Do not simply cut around a fungal lesion and use the remainder of the same affected leaflet. Prefer separate, fully healthy leaves after the disease has been controlled. Visual assessment cannot verify fungal toxins, microbial contamination, pesticide residues or the chemical potency of the material. Remove heavily infected leaves and fallen diseased debris and keep them away from healthy plant material. Improve airflow and reduce prolonged leaf wetness and overhead splash where practical. Sanitize pruning tools. Because this is a generic fungal label, confirm the disease before selecting a fungicide. If chemical control is needed, use only a fungicide registered locally for curry leaf/edible crops and the confirmed disease, and strictly follow the product label and pre-harvest interval.

Species: Neem
Disease Name: Alternaria
Display Name: Alternaria Leaf Spot / Blight
Description: A fungal foliar disease reported on neem and commonly associated with Alternaria alternata. It can begin as localized spotting and progress to blighting of affected leaf tissue.
Typical Symptoms: Dark brown to black spots, often on yellowing tissue; spots may enlarge or merge; affected areas can become necrotic and leaves may dry or shed prematurely.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy neem leaves are normally deep green and intact, without dark necrotic spots, expanding blighted areas, or abnormal yellowing.
Recommended Action: Treat visibly affected leaves as unsuitable for medicinal use. Remove severely affected material, reduce prolonged leaf wetness where practical, and obtain local plant-pathology confirmation before applying any treatment.

Species: Neem
Disease Name: Bacterial_infection
Display Name: Bacterial Leaf Infection
Description: A bacterial foliar condition of neem. Bacterial leaf spot and vein-blight symptoms have been reported in neem, with Pseudomonas and Xanthomonas species among reported causal bacteria.
Typical Symptoms: Water-soaked or angular dark spots, yellowing around lesions, vein blight, and sometimes shot-hole symptoms where dead tissue falls out; severe lesions may merge and contribute to leaf drop.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy neem leaves should not show water-soaked angular lesions, shot holes formed from dead tissue, dark bacterial-looking spots, or blighted veins.
Recommended Action: Do not use visibly affected leaves for medicinal preparation. Separate heavily affected material, avoid unnecessary overhead wetting, and have the condition confirmed before selecting a control method.

Species: Neem
Disease Name: Defoliator
Display Name: Defoliator Damage
Description: A pest-damage class rather than a plant disease. Defoliating insects or other leaf-feeding pests remove leaf tissue and can reduce foliage substantially.
Typical Symptoms: Missing or eaten leaf tissue, ragged or geometric chewing marks, partial skeletonization, damaged margins, drying of remaining tissue, and partial or severe defoliation.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy neem leaves retain complete leaflets and margins without extensive chewing, skeletonization, or unexplained loss of foliage.
Recommended Action: Do not use heavily pest-damaged leaves for medicinal preparation. Inspect both leaf surfaces and nearby shoots for feeding pests, remove severely damaged material, and identify the pest before choosing a control method.

Species: Neem
Disease Name: Dieback
Display Name: Neem Dieback
Description: A progressive shoot-and-branch decline syndrome reported in neem. Phomopsis azadirachtae has historically been associated with neem dieback in published reports.
Typical Symptoms: Drying starts at terminal shoots and progresses downward; crown thinning and defoliation may occur; inflorescence blight and premature wilting of fruits can accompany severe cases.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy neem shoots remain green and actively growing, without progressive drying from the tip toward the base or widespread branch death.
Recommended Action: Do not use leaves from actively dying or severely affected branches. Remove and isolate severely affected plant material where appropriate and seek plant-pathology confirmation because branch dieback can have multiple contributing causes.

Species: Neem
Disease Name: Irregular_yellowing
Display Name: Irregular Yellowing
Description: A visible stress/condition class, not a single confirmed disease diagnosis. Irregular yellowing in neem may be associated with nutritional deficiency, water/root stress, pest pressure, or systemic disease and therefore requires further diagnosis.
Typical Symptoms: Patchy or uneven chlorosis, yellowing of tips or margins, mottled green-and-yellow areas, reduced vigor, drooping, and in some cases premature leaf fall.
Healthy Plant Comparison: Healthy neem foliage is generally evenly green and vigorous rather than patchily chlorotic, mottled, or prematurely yellowing.
Recommended Action: Do not assign a pathogen from yellowing alone. Check nutrition, watering and drainage, roots, pest activity, and other disease signs. For medicinal-use screening, send strongly or unexplained yellow leaves for expert review.

Display Name: Leaf Blight
Description: A broad foliar blight syndrome in neem. Multiple fungal causes have been reported in different studies, so this class should not be treated as a single pathogen-specific diagnosis unless the dataset provides confirmed etiology.

Disease Name: Leaf_Miners
Display Name: Leaf Miner Damage
Description: An insect-mining damage class rather than a fungal or bacterial disease. Leaf-miner larvae feed within leaf tissue and create visible mines. The exact leaf-miner species on neem should be confirmed for the local dataset.

Species: centella_asiatica
Disease Name: Dried
Display Name: Dried Leaf Condition
Description: A low-moisture or post-harvest quality condition, not a disease diagnosis. A published Centella asiatica image dataset includes a Dried class, and dried Centella leaves are also intentionally prepared for products such as teas, decoctions and infusions.
